# Abstract

<!-- Draft by Li Yitong (with Claude Code), 2026-11-07. Check the simulation sentence after issue #21 (Section 5.2 main case at the measured contrast). -->

Short-video feeds are often said to trap users in "information cocoons", yet research and the "cocoon checks" that Chinese platforms launched in 2025 mostly measure how narrow a user's *topics* are. A user who watches many marriage videos may simply like the topic; the harm the idea describes is being shown, within the topic, mostly one hostile narrative. Little is known about this within-topic skew from the user's side: how common such narratives are, whether a recommender amplifies them, and whether a user can notice or undo it. We define a user's viewpoint skew separately from topic interest, split it exactly into a platform layer and a personal layer, and derive which user actions can reach each. We then study everyday social issues on Kuaishou with three kinds of evidence: a public log of 160,000 users labelled by language models, experiments on OneRec, a recommender trained on that log, and calibrated simulations. About 7% of impressions concern a social issue; within the five issue domains we study, about one in seven carries an opposing narrative; negative labels on a whole gender, our case study, appear in about 1% of marriage videos, and an open model overstates such narratives several-fold. OneRec responds strongly to topics but does not separate the group-label narrative from other videos on the issue, and its "not interested" signal raises similar content instead of lowering it. A user would need thousands of issue videos to detect a doubling of a rare narrative. The platform, which knows each user's exposure, is better placed than the user to measure and correct such skew.
